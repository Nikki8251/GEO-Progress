Pillar 1

Structure — make content extractable

AI systems extract passages, not pages. Every key claim should work as a standalone statement. The skill specifies 40–60 word answer blocks as the optimal extraction size.

Skill rule: Lead every section with a direct answer. Don't bury the conclusion. 40–60 words is the sweet spot for AI snippet extraction. Tables beat prose for comparison. Numbered lists beat paragraphs for process. Each paragraph: one idea.

Content block patterns — ZERO examples

Definition block — "What is X?" queries 45 words
ZERO Markets is a multi-regulated forex and CFD broker ← answer first licensed by ASIC (Australia), FMA (New Zealand), and FSC (Mauritius). ← regulatory proof immediately It offers raw ECN spreads from 0.0 pips on forex, indices, and commodities via MT4, MT5, and the ZEROx platform. ← specific, extractable facts
Comparison block — "X vs Y" queries 50 words
ZERO Markets vs Pepperstone: key differences ZERO Markets is regulated by ASIC + FMA + FSC. ← structured lead Pepperstone is regulated by ASIC + FCA + CySEC. Both offer raw ECN spreads from 0.0 pips. ZERO's minimum deposit: [X]. Pepperstone: $200. ← parallel structure — AI extracts this cleanly
FAQ block — "Is X safe?" queries 55 words
Q: Is ZERO Markets safe? A: Yes. ZERO Markets is regulated by ASIC ← direct answer first (Licence No. [X]), which requires client funds to be held in segregated accounts with tier-1 banks. ASIC's oversight includes mandatory dispute resolution and negative balance protection. ← specific, verifiable, citable
Statistic block — authority signal 40 words
ZERO Markets processes [X]% of withdrawals ← specific stat within 1 business day, based on internal processing data (Q1 2026). ← source + date = +40% visibility The industry average is 2–3 business days according to Finance Magnates (2025). ← external citation = +37% more boost

What Not To Do

Common mistakes
applied to ZERO

The skill lists 12 mistakes that hurt AI visibility. Each mapped to ZERO's current situation — some we're already making, some we're at risk of making.

  • 🔴
    No structured data — we're missing this
    Schema markup deploying now. Without it, AI can't parse our regulatory info or FAQ answers. Priority: Organization + FAQPage first.
  • 🔴
    Potentially blocking AI bots in robots.txt
    Not yet audited. If GPTBot or PerplexityBot are blocked, those platforms literally cannot cite us. Audit and fix immediately.
  • 🔴
    No freshness signals — undated content
    AI systems weight recency. Our testing found "outdated" notes on Minimum deposit and Withdrawal speed answers. Adding "Last updated" dates is a 1-hour fix.
  • 🟠
    Generic content without data — "We're the best"
    Product pages use marketing language. AI cites specifics: "spreads from 0.0 pips on EUR/USD" not "ultra-competitive pricing". Audit and rewrite core pages.
  • 🟠
    Pricing behind JS / not machine-readable
    AI agents comparing brokers on behalf of buyers can't parse our accounts page if it's JS-rendered. Deploy /pricing.md as a static, readable file.
  • 🟠
    Keyword stuffing on product pages
    Princeton study: keyword stuffing actively reduces AI visibility by 10%. If product pages repeat "forex broker" / "CFD broker" excessively, clean it up.
  • 🟡
    Ignoring third-party presence
    We may get more AI citations from a Investopedia mention than from our own blog. WikiFX at 7× citations vs zeromarkets.com at 5× proves this is already happening.
  • 🟡
    Not monitoring — no Round 2 test scheduled
    May 2025 was Round 1. Without a regular monitoring cadence, we can't measure the impact of changes. Set up monthly DIY test or subscribe to Otterly/Peec AI.
  • 🟢
    Writing separate content "for AI" — we're not doing this
    Good. Google's guide explicitly warns against this. Our strategy is structural optimisation of existing content, not AI-bait fragments.
  • 🟢
    Gating authoritative content — not a current issue
    Core pages (regulation, accounts, FAQ) are public. AI can access them. Keep it this way — don't gate regulatory content behind sign-up walls.
